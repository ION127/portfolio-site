I took part in BARO across the board, from planning to deployment and operations, in close discussion with my teammates. What follows is the code I personally contributed: between late April and late June 2026 I merged 128 pull requests across six repositories.

### Getting vehicle positions to the screen and to dispatch

- I reshaped the control service, which receives vehicle positions, into a single MQTT → Kafka path. I removed its early Redis and PostGIS dependencies and added an endpoint that keeps vehicle state in memory and pushes it to the control console over SSE.
- I fixed MQTT session and inbound-client issues and a 404 on the ALB path (`/control`), and added trip status and plate numbers to the position messages.
- I made the Kafka consumers store trip status with the vehicle data, and made each consumer switchable per environment.

### Dispatch and relocation

- I made vehicles that haven’t reported for over 30 seconds drop out of the dispatch candidates (Redis lastSeen), and made an unacknowledged dispatch go to the next car.
- I built the flow that automatically relocates a vehicle to a high-demand area after a trip. A relocating vehicle can still be dispatched if a request comes in.
- I split the admin routes in the gateway and made it check admin rights against the role claim in the JWT.

### Control console

- I built the real-time fleet control console: vehicle positions, trip routes and relocating vehicles (purple markers with their destinations) on a map, behind an admin login with JWT.
- I also fixed the rider app map breaking when the window was resized or the user zoomed.

### Infrastructure and delivery (Terraform)

- In Terraform, I built the Site-to-Site VPN between the baro-dev VPC and the on-prem network, moved Kafka from ECS to EC2 with an attached EBS volume (Cloud Map DNS), and replaced AWS IoT Core with Mosquitto on EC2.
- I built the ECR build and ECS deploy pipeline for the control service, and a step that deploys the vehicle simulator over SSM after `terraform apply`.
- To cut cost, I added an automatic RDS snapshot before teardown with automatic restore on the next apply, moved Kafka’s EC2 to t3.small, removed the bastion (SSM only) and lowered CloudWatch log costs. Kafka's EBS volume is protected from accidental deletion.

### Fixes while scaling to 1,500 vehicles

- Removing a per-message DB lookup from the hot path cleared a 2.9M consumer lag ([incident](#incident-baro-consumer-lag)).
- When Kafka stalled, the producer's wait blocked MQTT intake too; I cut that wait short ([incident](#incident-baro-kafka-block)).
- Mosquitto's queue overflowed and dropped messages; broker queue settings and staggered simulator connections (0.05 s every 10 vehicles) stopped it ([incident](#incident-baro-mosquitto-queue)).
